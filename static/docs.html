Harvard · First-Year Seminar Program · Handbook

A handbook for The Packet Creator

Each year the FYSP office produces a folder of reports — a “packet” — for every academic department that offers a first-year seminar. This tool takes three spreadsheets, makes the seven reports for every department in seconds, and bundles them as PDFs ready to send.

I.

What this is

The First-Year Seminar Program packets historically required someone in the FYSP office to assemble, by hand, a folder of reports for each of the forty-odd departments at Harvard. This tool automates that — it reads the office's working spreadsheets and produces every department's packet at once.

You do not need to write code, install Python, or understand how the reports are generated. You only need three things: the source workbooks, a browser, and someone (or this tool's host) keeping the application running.

Who this handbook is for FYSP office staff who run the tool once or twice a year. We assume no programming background; we do assume you've worked with the office's Excel files before, or that you can ask whoever currently maintains them.
II.

What it produces

For each department — for instance [ECON] (Economics), [HLS] (Harvard Law School), [SEAS] (Engineering and Applied Sciences) — the tool produces a folder containing up to seven PDFs and one combined PDF that stitches them together.

III.

The three workbooks

Everything the tool knows comes from these three Excel files. They are not public exports from a Harvard system that you download once and use; they are internal FYSP office files, assembled and curated by FYSP staff each year. Any one of them is sufficient to produce the reports it covers — the others will simply be skipped.

FileFriendly nameWhere it comes from
SAVED.xlsx Faculty Teaching History Internal FYSP file, updated each year by office staff.
APPLICATION_CURRENT.xlsx Current-Year Applications Pulled in part from the FYSP application portal, compiled by office staff each fall.
HOLYGRAIL.xlsx Prior-Year Evaluations Compiled by office staff from the prior year's Q-report scores and enrollment.

Faculty Teaching History

SAVED.xlsx

Produces PDFs 1, 2, 4, 5

A multi-year ledger of which faculty member taught a first-year seminar in which year, in which department, at what rank. It is the most complex of the three files and the only one that spans more than a single year.

Where it lives

Inside the FYSP office. It is not downloaded from any external system — it is a working document maintained by office staff. When you join the office, ask whoever last ran the packet generation for the current copy.

What you must do to it each year

Before generating packets for a new academic year, the file needs the new year's information added by hand:

  • Add a new column to the right for the incoming year, named like 2025-26 or 2025-2026 (the tool will abbreviate it on the PDF).
  • For each faculty member, mark whether they are teaching a first-year seminar that year using the notation system below.
  • Update ranks. If a professor was promoted (Assistant → Associate → Professor), retired (→ Emeritus / Emerita), or left, update the Rank column so this year's rank is accurate. Joint appointments are usually written as Assistant Professor / Associate Professor separated by a slash.
  • Add new hires. If a department brought in new faculty teaching a seminar this year, add a new row.

Required columns

Column headerWhat goes in it
ProfessorFaculty name (free-form text).
RankCurrent rank — see notes on joint appointments below.
DepartmentDepartment name spelled out — for example, Economics, not ECON. The tool maps full names to acronyms internally.
One column per academic yearFor example 2014-15, 2015-16, …, 2025-26. Each cell uses the notation below to mark how that person taught that year.

Notation in the year cells

SymbolMeaning
XTaught one section that year.
XXTaught two sections that year.
X*Joint instruction or co-teaching — counted as half a seminar.
X**, X***Three- or four-way co-teaching — also counted as half (the share-out is handled in aggregate).
X!For joint appointments — the lower rank in the slash gets credit. Example: Assistant Professor / Associate Professor with X! credits Assistant.
X!!For joint appointments — the higher rank gets credit.
X*!Joint co-teaching and joint appointment — half credit to the lower rank.
(blank)Did not teach that year.
The cardinal rule The integrity of every report from this file depends on these notations being correct. When in doubt, leave it blank rather than guess — a missing seminar is easier to notice than a miscredited one.

Current-Year Applications

APPLICATION_CURRENT.xlsx

Produces PDF 6 — Current-Year Enrollment Report

A roster of every first-year seminar being offered this academic year, together with how many students applied and how many were ultimately placed. It is the basis for the “popularity” story the packet tells each department: how oversubscribed (or undersubscribed) their seminars were this year.

Where it comes from

Partly the FYSP application portal — student applications are exported from there after the placement deadline. Historically the FYSP office has further edited and consolidated the export into a single working spreadsheet (Nina has been the person doing this in recent years). Ask the office for the current copy or the export-and-clean procedure that produces it.

What “# of Apps” vs. “Enrolled” mean

  • Total Appl Count (“# of Apps”) is how many students applied for the seminar. Because students rank multiple seminars, this number can be higher than the number who actually take the course.
  • Placed (“Enrolled”) is how many students were placed in the seminar after the lottery — measured as of the fall course registration deadline.

Sem#Seminar number, e.g. 15K, 32T.
TermFall or Spring.

FnameInstructor first name.
LNameInstructor last name.

Cancelled or unrun seminars Rows containing NO PACKET in any column are dropped automatically — use this convention if you need to keep a row in the file but exclude it from reports.

Prior-Year Evaluations

HOLYGRAIL.xlsx

Produces PDF 7 — Prior-Year Enrollment & Evaluations

A consolidated record of last year's first-year seminars: how many students applied, how many enrolled, and how students rated the seminar and instructor on the Q-report. This is what departments use to judge how their seminars actually went.

Where it comes from

This file is assembled by the FYSP office at the end of each academic year. It draws from two sources:

  • Q-report evaluation scores for each seminar — the student-completed course evaluation system.
  • Enrollment numbers from the prior year's APPLICATION_CURRENT.xlsx (this year's “HOLYGRAIL” is largely last year's “APPLICATION_CURRENT” with Q-scores merged in).

Historically Nina has compiled this. If you are taking over the role, ask for the previous year's file and the procedure used to fold Q-scores in.

A note on the name The filename HOLYGRAIL.xlsx is historic and inscrutable. A future maintainer is welcome to rename it (e.g. EVALUATIONS_PRIOR.xlsx) — but doing so requires a small developer change in two places in the code (see § VIII). Until then, the tool expects the file to be uploaded into the “Prior-Year Evaluations” slot regardless of its actual filename.
IV.

Running the tool

Once the three workbooks are prepared, the process itself is fast — under a minute end-to-end. The application runs in your web browser; you do not need to install anything.

  1. Open the application. Go to the URL the office has set up (typically http://localhost:5050 if you are running it on your own machine, or a Harvard-hosted URL otherwise). If the page does not load, see § VI.
  2. Upload your workbooks. Drag each file onto its labeled box, or click and browse. You can upload one, two, or all three; missing files just skip their reports. The tool accepts .xlsx and .xls.
  3. Click “Compose packets”. The button only becomes active once at least one file is loaded. Generation takes ten to thirty seconds depending on how many departments are in your files.
  4. Browse the index of departments. Every department with data will appear in a list, with a count of how many PDFs were produced. You can search by code (e.g. ECON) or full name, and sort by code, name, or PDF count.
  5. Preview each PDF in the browser. Click any PDF in a department row to open it in the side panel without downloading. Click “Preview combined” to see the stitched-together packet for a department. Press Esc or “Close ×” to dismiss.
  6. Download. Either download a single department's ZIP from its row, or use “Download all (ZIP)” at the top of the index to grab every department at once. The ZIP preserves the [DEPT]/ folder structure.
Re-running You can re-run with different files at any time — the previous output is cleared automatically. Use the “Clear” button if you want to start over without uploading.

VI.

Troubleshooting

The page won't load

The application needs to be running on a server. If you are running it locally, open a terminal in the project folder and type python app.py — leave that window open while you use the tool. If it is supposed to be hosted, contact whoever set up the deployment.

Generation finished but a department is missing

The most common cause is a spelling difference in the Department column. The tool matches department names against an internal list (e.g. Government → GOV, Economics → ECON). If a department name is misspelled or abbreviated in your spreadsheet, the tool will still create a folder for it but may not match it to the proper acronym. Check the “Warnings” shelf at the bottom of the results page for clues.

A PDF says “No data available”

The tool found a department in one workbook but no matching rows in the workbook for that PDF. Example: PDF 6 says “no data” for [ANTH] — that means SAVED.xlsx had Anthropology, but APPLICATION_CURRENT.xlsx had no Anthropology rows this year. This is expected if a department is not running a seminar this year.

A warning appears about reading a workbook

Open the file in Excel and check the column headers against the tables in § III. The most common culprits are a renamed header (e.g. Department Name instead of Department) or a stray blank column.

Faculty appear in the wrong rank category

Check the Rank column in SAVED.xlsx. The categorization rules are listed in the Legend on the bottom of PDF 4. Common surprises: Senior Lecturer, Senior Preceptor, Professor of the Practice, and Professor in Residence are all grouped together as SL/SP/PoP/PiR. Anything with Visiting in the title goes to Visiting (all levels).

VII.

Yearly maintenance

Before each year's packet run, the office's working files need attention. Use this as a checklist.

  1. Update SAVED.xlsx for the new year. Add a new year column on the right. Fill in the X / XX / X* / X! / X!! notation for every faculty member. Promote, retire, or remove faculty whose status changed. Add new hires.
  2. Produce APPLICATION_CURRENT.xlsx for the new year. Export student applications and placements from the FYSP application portal once the lottery is finalized. Clean and merge into the canonical sheet shape (see § III).
  3. Produce HOLYGRAIL.xlsx for the prior year. Take last year's APPLICATION_CURRENT.xlsx, merge in the matching Q-report scores for each seminar, and save as the new HOLYGRAIL.xlsx. This is the most time-consuming step.
  4. Ask a developer to update hardcoded year titles, if needed. The PDF 6 and PDF 7 titles include literal year ranges (e.g. “2025–2026 Enrollment Report”). When a new academic year begins, those strings must be updated in two files. See § VIII.
  5. Run the tool, review, and distribute. Generate packets, spot-check a few departments (especially any with unusual joint appointments), then download and email out.
VIII.

For developers

If you are taking over maintenance of the tool itself, this section is for you.

Tech stack

  • Backend — Python 3, Flask, pandas, openpyxl, reportlab, PyPDF2.
  • Frontend — vanilla HTML / CSS / JS. No build step. Served by Flask from static/ and templates/.

Running locally

From the project root:

pip install -r requirements.txt
python app.py

The app starts at http://localhost:5050.

Yearly code changes

  1. Bump the PDF 6 title year. In generators/pdf6_enrollment.py, find the line containing 2025–2026 Enrollment Report and update it to the new year. (Note the dash is a Unicode en-dash, not a hyphen.)
  2. Bump the PDF 7 title year. In generators/pdf7_evaluations.py, do the same for 2024–2025 Enrollment and Evaluations.
  3. (Optional) Refactor those out to constants. A small future cleanup: move both year strings into generators/shared.py as CURRENT_YEAR_LABEL and PRIOR_YEAR_LABEL so the office can update them without touching generator code.

Renaming HOLYGRAIL.xlsx

To rename the file users upload (e.g. to EVALUATIONS_PRIOR.xlsx):

  • In app.py, change the holygrail_xlsx field key and the saved filename. Two occurrences.
  • In static/app.js, change the SOURCES entry for holygrail so its field matches the new backend key.
  • In templates/index.html, update the dropzone's data-field attribute and the displayed filename.
  • The internals (column names like DEPT, APPL#, SEMQ) do not need to change — the rename is purely cosmetic at the upload boundary.

Adding a new department

Open generators/shared.py and add an entry to DEPARTMENT_MAPPING: the key is the full name as it appears in your workbooks, the value is the acronym used for folder names and codes.
