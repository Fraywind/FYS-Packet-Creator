Harvard. First-Year Seminar Program. Staff guide

How to Create the Department Packets

Each year the FYSP office puts together a folder of reports, called a “packet,” for every academic department that offers a first-year seminar. This tool takes three Excel files and builds every department’s packet at once. These pages walk you through getting those files ready and running the tool. You do not need any coding background.

1.

What this tool does

FYSP packets used to be put together by hand: one folder of reports per department, forty-something departments, every year. This tool does that work automatically. You give it three Excel files that the FYSP office already maintains, and it produces every department’s packet in about a minute.

The three files:

  • Faculty Teaching History (FYSP Master.xlsx). Who taught what, year by year. Produces PDFs 1, 2, 4, 5.
  • Current Year Seminars (FYSP Current Year Seminars.xlsx). This year’s seminars and how popular they were. Produces PDF 6.
  • Past Year Enrollment and Q Reports (FYSP Past Year Enrollment and Q Reports.xlsx). Last year’s enrollment and student evaluations. Produces PDF 7.
Who this is for FYSP office staff who run the packets each year, and anyone new who is stepping into that role. The office is small, so this guide is written assuming you may be doing this for the first time. If you get stuck, the person who last ran the packets is the best place to start.
2.

For each department, for example [ECON] (Economics), [HLS] (Harvard Law School), or [SEAS] (Engineering and Applied Sciences), the tool produces a folder with up to seven PDFs, plus one combined PDF that stitches them together into a single file ready to send.

3.

The three input files

Everything the tool knows comes from these three Excel files. They are not public exports you download from a Harvard system. They are internal FYSP office files, assembled and curated by office staff each year.

You can run the tool with all three files, or with only one or two. Any file you leave out simply means its reports are skipped, which is handy when you only want to refresh part of the packet.

FileWhat we call it hereWhere it comes from

FYSP Current Year Seminars.xlsx Current Year Seminars Pulled from the FYSP application portal, compiled by office staff each fall. Request HCIR for this data file.
FYSP Past Year Enrollment and Q Reports.xlsx Past Year Enrollment and Q Reports Compiled by office staff from the prior year’s Q report scores and enrollment. Request HCIR for this data file.

Faculty Teaching History

FYSP Master.xlsx

Produces PDFs 1, 2, 4, 5

A multi-year record of which faculty member taught a first-year seminar in which year, in which department, at what rank. This is the only file that spans more than a single year, and the most complex of the three.

Where to find it

This file stays inside the FYSP office. You do not download it from anywhere. It is a working spreadsheet that office staff keep up to date by hand. If you are taking over, ask whoever last ran the packets for the current copy.

What you do to it each year

Before generating packets for a new academic year, the file needs the new year’s information added (almost always by hand):

  • Add a new year column on the right, named like 2025-26 or 2025-2026. The tool will abbreviate it on the PDFs.
  • For each faculty member, mark whether they are teaching a first-year seminar that year using the notation below.
  • Update ranks. If a professor was promoted (for example, from Assistant Professor to Associate Professor to full Professor), retired (to Emeritus or Emerita), or left, update the Rank column so this year’s rank is correct.
  • Track rank progression for our own records. If someone started teaching for FYSP as an Assistant Professor and was promoted later, you will see ranks written with a slash, like Assistant Professor / Associate Professor. The slash means they started at the first rank and are now at the second. It is FYSP’s own shorthand, not a joint appointment.
  • Add new hires. If a department brought in new faculty teaching a seminar this year, add a new row.
Updating ranks is the slowest step Going through every faculty row to check promotions, retirements, and departures takes longer than anything else here. Two things make it quicker. First, you only really need to update the people teaching this year; everyone else can wait until they teach again. Second, you can ask HCIR for a list of faculty whose rank changed over the past year, then use that list to update the Rank column instead of checking each person one by one.

Current Year Seminars

FYSP Current Year Seminars.xlsx

Produces PDF 6. Current Year Enrollment Report

A roster of every first-year seminar offered this academic year, with how many students applied and how many were placed. The basis for the popularity story the packet tells each department: which seminars were oversubscribed, which were not.

When it is ready

Usually once the course registration deadline has passed. Before that deadline the placement numbers are still moving, so a packet built early will not match the final enrollment.

Where it comes from

It comes partly from the FYSP application portal. Student applications are exported from there after the placement deadline, and the office then edits and consolidates that export into a single working spreadsheet. Request this data file from HCIR.

What “Total Appl Count” vs. “Placed” mean

  • Total Appl Count (also “# of Apps”) is how many students applied for the seminar. Because students rank multiple seminars, this number can be higher than the number who actually take the course.
  • Placed (also “Enrolled”) is how many students were placed in the seminar after the lottery, measured at the fall course registration deadline.

Past Year Enrollment and Q Reports

FYSP Past Year Enrollment and Q Reports.xlsx

Produces PDF 7. Prior Year Enrollment and Evaluations

A consolidated record of last year’s first-year seminars: how many students applied, how many enrolled, and how students rated each seminar and instructor on the Q report. This is what departments look at to judge how their seminars actually went.

Where it comes from

Assembled by the FYSP office at the end of each academic year. It draws from two sources:

  • Q report evaluation scores for each seminar, from the student course evaluation system.
  • Enrollment numbers from the prior year’s Current Year Seminars file. In effect, this year’s Past Year Enrollment and Q Reports is largely last year’s Current Year Seminars with Q scores merged in.

Request HCIR for this data file.

4.

Running the tool

Once the three files are ready, the rest is quick, usually under a minute from start to finish. The tool runs in your web browser, so there is nothing to install beyond starting the server (ask whoever set the tool up for you if you are not sure how).

  1. Open the application. Go to the URL the office has set up for the tool. If the page does not load, see section 6.
  2. Upload your files. Drag each file onto its labeled box, or click to browse. You can upload one, two, or all three. Missing files just skip their reports. The tool accepts .xlsx and .xls.
  3. Click “Compose packets”. The button becomes active once at least one file is loaded. Generation takes ten to thirty seconds depending on how many departments are in your files.
  4. Browse the department list. Every department with data appears in a list, with a count of how many PDFs were produced. You can search by code (e.g. ECON) or full name, and sort by code, name, or PDF count.
  5. Preview each PDF in the browser. Click any PDF in a department row to open it in the side panel without downloading. Click “Preview combined” to see the stitched-together packet for a department. Press Esc to close.
  6. Download. Either download a single department’s ZIP from its row, or use “Download all (ZIP)” at the top of the index to grab every department at once. The ZIP preserves the [DEPT]/ folder structure.
Re-running You can re-run with different files at any time. The previous output is cleared automatically. Use the “Clear” button if you want to start over without uploading.

6.

When something looks wrong

The page won’t load

The tool needs to be running on a server. If it is hosted by the office, contact whoever set up the hosting. If you are running it on your own machine, ask that person to start it for you, or follow the start instructions they gave you. Leave that window open while you use the tool.

Generation finished but a department is missing

The most common cause is a spelling difference in the Department column. The tool matches department names against an internal list (for instance, Government maps to GOV; Economics maps to ECON). If a department name is misspelled or abbreviated in your spreadsheet, the tool will still create a folder for it but may not match it to the proper acronym. Check the “Warnings” shelf at the bottom of the results page for clues.

A PDF says “No data available”

The tool found a department in one file but no matching rows in the file for that PDF. For example, PDF 6 says “no data” for [ANTH]. That means Faculty Teaching History had Anthropology, but Current Year Seminars had no Anthropology rows this year. This is expected when a department is not running a seminar this year.

A warning appears about reading a file

Open the file in Excel and check the column headers against the tables in section 3. The most common culprits are a renamed header (for example, Department Name instead of Department) or a stray blank column.

Faculty appear in the wrong rank category

Check the Rank column in Faculty Teaching History. The categorization rules are listed in the Legend on the bottom of PDF 4. Common surprises: Senior Lecturer, Senior Preceptor, Professor of the Practice, and Professor in Residence are all grouped together. Anything with Visiting in the title goes to Visiting (all levels).

7.

Each year, before generating

Before each year’s packet run, the office’s working files need attention. Use this as a checklist.

  1. Update Faculty Teaching History for the new year. Add a new year column on the right. Fill in the X, XX, X*, X!, X!! notation for every faculty member who taught. Promote, retire, or remove faculty whose status changed, and add any new hires. This is the slowest step, so consider updating only the faculty teaching this year first and revisiting the rest later. You can also ask HCIR for a list of faculty whose rank changed, which saves you from checking each person by hand.
  2. Get Current Year Seminars for the new year. Once the lottery is finalized, request the data file from HCIR. Clean and merge it into the same column shape described in section 3.
  3. Get Past Year Enrollment and Q Reports for the prior year. Take last year’s Current Year Seminars file, merge in the Q report scores for each seminar (request these from HCIR), and save as the new Past Year Enrollment and Q Reports file. This is the most time-consuming step.
  4. Ask whoever maintains the tool to update the year titles, if needed. The PDF 6 and PDF 7 titles include literal year ranges (for example, “2025 to 2026 Enrollment Report”). When a new academic year begins, those titles have to be updated. It is a small change in the tool’s code, so it is not something you need to do yourself, but it is worth flagging to whoever maintains the tool.
  5. Run the tool, review, and distribute. Generate packets, spot-check a few departments (especially any with unusual slashed ranks), then download and email out.
